Merton Jump Diffusion
The Massive Upgrade to Black-Scholes: Super Simplified

One-sentence version: Black-Scholes is continuous random walk. Merton adds sudden jumps (crashes, surprises). You still get a closed-form formula, which is just a weighted average of Black-Scholes prices.

Why Black-Scholes Is Incomplete

  • Assumes prices move smoothly and continuously
  • Cannot produce fat tails or volatility smiles
  • Fails during crashes and big news events

Merton's Brilliant Idea (1976)

Keep the continuous geometric Brownian motion, but add a compound Poisson jump process:

\[ \frac{dS_t}{S_{t-}} = (r - \lambda \bar{k})\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t + (Y-1)\,dN_t \]
  • \(N_t\) = Poisson process with intensity \(\lambda\) (expected jumps per year)
  • \(Y\) = jump multiplier, \(\ln Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_J, \sigma_J^2)\)
  • \(\bar{k} = \mathbb{E}[Y-1] = e^{\mu_J + \frac12\sigma_J^2} - 1\) (compensator so discounted stock is a martingale)

The Closed-Form Magic

Conditional on exactly \(n\) jumps, the stock is still log-normal → Black-Scholes still works, just with adjusted volatility and rate.

\[ C = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-\lambda' T}(\lambda' T)^n}{n!}\, \text{BS}(S, K, T, r_n, \sigma_n) \]
where \[ \lambda' = \lambda(1+\bar{k}), \quad \sigma_n = \sqrt{\sigma^2 + \frac{n\sigma_J^2}{T}}, \quad r_n = r - \lambda\bar{k} + \frac{n(\mu_J + \tfrac12\sigma_J^2)}{T} \]

In practice you only need the first 20–50 terms. Extremely fast.

Super Simple Python Implementation

from merton import merton_call

price = merton_call(
    S=100, K=100, T=1.0, r=0.05,
    sigma=0.2,          # continuous vol
    lam=1.0,            # jumps per year
    mu_j=-0.10,         # mean log-jump
    sigma_j=0.30        # jump volatility
)
print(f"Merton Call: {price:.4f}")

No external dependencies beyond the standard library. See merton.py for the full clean code.

Typical Effect

With the parameters above you usually get a higher call price than pure Black-Scholes. The difference is the jump risk premium: you cannot perfectly hedge jumps, so options cost more.

Parameters You Control

  • \(\sigma\) — continuous (diffusion) volatility
  • \(\lambda\) — how often jumps happen
  • \(\mu_J\) — average size/direction of jumps (negative = crash bias)
  • \(\sigma_J\) — how variable the jump sizes are

When to Use This

  • You want better smiles and fat tails than Black-Scholes
  • You need a closed form (not Monte Carlo or FFT)
  • You want something simple enough to explain and implement in an afternoon
